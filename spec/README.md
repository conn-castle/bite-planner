# Ordering spec

What the ordering feature must do, and how it should look.

- [`requirements.md`](requirements.md): the functional requirements, as a checklist. Check items off as they're implemented.
- [`mockup.html`](mockup.html): the UI, as a clickable mockup with mock data. Open it in a browser; the panel on the left switches between home states. It is the design reference, not code to reuse. Delete it once the app implements it.

Where the two disagree, the requirements win. Decisions from reviews of the built app also win over the mockup:

- Every row of days is one shared component (`apps/mobile/src/ui/DateBar`), configured with props (e.g. the compact strip on home) but sharing the same parts.
- The delivery date's day row starts at the last day the previous order's meals cover, or at today if that's later; today is shown greyed out and labeled Today.
- The flow's steps each do one thing: Pick meals, Servings, Delivery date, Next delivery, Shopping list (waiting for it if needed, then reviewing it), and a final review that places the order (from piece 9, approves it). This replaces the single "Dates and servings" step decided earlier (built in step 5 of the build order), and adds Servings and the final review to the mockup's steps. The shopping list starts building as the member leaves Servings. (Piece 9 moves the shopping list to right after Servings: see the shopped list below.)
- The next-delivery day row marks the days that have meals with a bar in the order's color, starts at the delivery day, and still marks the best day.
- Placed order cards (home and the order view) have a 1px border in the order's color at 40% (`color-mix(in srgb, var(--c) 40%, transparent)`), not the mockup's neutral hairline, so each card matches its bar in the day strip.
- The bar of an order not delivered yet is solid, in a lighter tint of the order's color (45% of it on white), not dashed as in the mockup.
- While meal ideas are being made, the draft card shows how long it has taken ("Working for 0:42") instead of the mockup's made-up estimate ("About 1 min left").
- The draft card doesn't credit picks to a person ("2 meals added", not "Sam has added 2 meals"): anyone in the household can pick, and the order records only who started it.
- A placed card's last track node (its days) shows no progress ring before the first day; the mockup's empty ring still draws its round line end as a dot.

- A meal's rating is one of four exclusive values, worst to best: "not for us" (the ✕ while picking), thumbs down, thumbs up, and favorite (a favorite is always liked, so it can't be thumbs down). No rating is the fifth state. Discarding a draft keeps its "not for us" marks.
- While picking, a suggestion has only the ✕. Thumbs and the heart are for meals of placed orders.

The mockup predates two requirements: it has no meal ratings (thumbs up, thumbs down, favorite, and the prompt to rate last week's meals), and it shows food illustrations where the app shows an icon. Their design was chosen in review:

- A meal's page has one round button at the end of its toolbar: "?" when not rated, otherwise the rating. Tapped, it grows sideways into the three choices (thumbs down, thumbs up, heart); tapping the chosen one clears it.
- The prompt to rate last week's meals is on the "Finding meals" step while suggestions generate: one card per order, in the look of History's order cards, titled "How were these meals?", with the three choices beside each meal.
- History has a search field and filter chips (All, Favorites, Liked, Didn't like, Not rated) under the "By order" / "All meals" switch, and each meal shows its rating as a small mark.

Settings, as reviewed:

- The mockup's Dislikes row isn't built, and won't be: ratings and "not for us" cover it.
- Members is a read-only row listing everyone in the household ("You, Sam"), with no chevron; adding and removing members isn't scheduled yet.
- Default servings is a stepper (1–12), like the decide-by and reminder days, not the mockup's placeholder row.
- The Household group starts with the household's Name; Account has Your name and Email. A name changes in a small dialog.
- Settings has no preview of the next order's dates (the mockup's "With these settings, Order N"): settings hold settings.
- The "By order" / "All meals" switch's track is a tint of ink (8%), not the mockup's `--cc-fill-strong`, which is all but the color of the warm page behind it.

Notifications, as decided:

- "Your meal ideas are ready" goes only to the member who started the batch.
- Tapping a notification opens the order flow only when the household's draft is the one it's about (a reminder's order, or the draft whose ideas are ready); otherwise it opens Home with an alert saying why (already placed, not started yet, or the draft was discarded), and Home's top card has the next step. A tap never starts a draft (the mockup opens the flow either way).
- In the app, iOS shows no banner; "ideas ready" shows as the mockup's toast unless the order flow is open. Reminders show nothing extra (Home already shows the order's state).
- Settings' Personal group has a "Daily reminders" switch (each member's own, kept by the server; on by default): off, that member gets no daily reminders. "Your meal ideas are ready" has no switch: it goes only to whoever asked for the ideas. While iOS doesn't allow Castle Chef's notifications on the phone, the group starts with a row saying so and a button: Allow (iOS's prompt, the first time) or Open Settings (after that). The mockup's "Allow notifications: On" row isn't built.
- Where the mockup says "yours at 8:00 AM", a member whose reminders are off (the switch, or iOS) sees "yours are off" (the draft card: "your reminders are off").
- The Next delivery step's card has no mini strip of days (the mockup's `.mini-strip`): the day row under it already marks the days the meals cover.
- The Next delivery step says what it sets for the following order in one line ("Order 5: decide by …, reminders start …"), not the mockup's "What this sets" group.

The shopping list, as built (stages 1 and 2; the mockup has the list step but not its waiting, its on-hand items, or changing amounts; in the flow, the shopped list below replaces its items, and a placed order from before piece 9 still shows them):

- While the list is made, its step shows the same waiting screen as "Finding meals" (the orb, how long it has taken, Notify me), titled "Making your shopping list". "Your shopping list is ready" goes to whoever left Servings, and its tap opens the flow at the list.
- Items assumed on hand are behind an info button under the list's summary ("ⓘ 3 items assumed on hand"); tapping it opens a small dialog in the middle of the screen (ui/InfoDialog) listing the ones these recipes use, closed with Got it.
- The list has a sixth aisle, Other (after Pantry), for items no other aisle fits; the mockup has five.
- Each aisle is a card with a thin border on the white page, its head a band in the aisle's own soft color with its name and count ("Produce … 4"); the mockup has a small grey heading with the count at the far right. The aisle colors are their own set (theme/tokens.css), apart from the order colors. It leaves room for a product picture at the start of each row later (stage 3).
- In the list step, each item ends with its amount's stepper (whole steps for things counted, ¼ or ½ for small amounts in units) and a red ×, not the mockup's amount at the start and − / Restore. The × is red with no background; tapping it crosses the whole item out (its name in red) and greys out its stepper at the amount it had, and tapping it again puts the item back. Once an amount was changed, the line under the item's name says "Recipes need 2¼ lb · Reset" (in place of its meals, so nothing moves), and Reset puts it back to that amount (the server keeps both). The controls stay at the row's end, where iOS puts a row's controls (Apple's guidance leads a row with its picture or text). A placed order's list (read-only) keeps the mockup's amount at the start.
- The list's summary is "to order", "removed", and the delivery day, as mocked.
- The Review step (not in the mockup) starts with the order as a card in its color ("Ready to place", when it arrives, the days it covers, the counts of meals, servings, and items, and the next order's dates, which aren't decided yet: "Order 5 would need to arrive by Fri 16 · decide by Thu 15"; no meal pictures, since the meals are listed under it), then its meals, dates, and shopping list as rows that go back to change them.
- A recipe opened from Pick meals, picked or not, has no servings stepper: its amounts are as written, its Ingredients heading has an asterisk, and a footnote under the list (ui/Note) says how many servings the recipe is for and that it's scaled to the servings chosen in the next step; servings are chosen only in the Servings step (Nick, 2026-10-11, issue #3).

The shopping list shopped at Instacart (stage 3, piece 9; the mockup has none of it), as reviewed in the design (2026-09-27) and replacing the list step's items above in the flow:

- A card's × (a pick's "don't show meals like this", a product's "don't buy this") is one component (ui/CornerToggle): red, on a white circle a little off the card's top-right corner (reviewed 2026-09-28, over the mockup's grey × inside the pick card's picture); while it's off (a pick marked "not for us", a product not bought) it shows ↺ there instead, to put it back.
- A recipe's servings go back to what was chosen (an order's servings, else the recipe's own) with a reset icon to the left of the stepper, shown only once they've been changed (reviewed 2026-10-04, over "Reset to 4" under the label, which showed whenever they differed from the recipe's).
- A pushed page (a recipe, a product) always shows its title in the toolbar, not only once its heading scrolls away (reviewed 2026-09-28).
- On wide screens (700px and up), the pick step's meals and the shopping list's product tiles are four across instead of two (reviewed 2026-09-28).
- Continuing an order opens the flow at the furthest step it has reached, by anyone in the household, not at picking meals (as mocked); the member can go back from there (Nick, 2026-09-30). The order keeps that step; when the order no longer supports it (say the picks changed since the list), the flow opens at the step that comes first, as it does mid-flow.
- The whole order flow is full-screen over the app at every screen size (not Ionic's centered dialog on wide screens), closed with its × (reviewed 2026-09-28, over the mockup's card that slides up over the page and closes with a swipe down): it's a long task, and a stray swipe mustn't close it.
- The flow is Pick meals, Servings, Shopping list, Delivery, Next delivery, Review: the list comes right after Servings, so shopping starts as early as it can. The list step's waiting screen covers making the list ("Making your shopping list") and then shopping it ("Shopping at Wegmans"); "Your shopping list is ready" goes out once it's shopped ("12 products for Order 5. 1 item to decide on.").
- The list step shows the Wegmans products chosen for the list, by aisle, in the list's aisle cards; the list of things to buy isn't shown in the flow any more. Each product is a tile, two across (reviewed 2026-09-28, over rows): its picture on top, centered; the red × a little off the tile's top corner (tap it to not buy the product, again to buy it after all); the item from the list in bold ("Celery stalks"), and under it the product in small grey text (cut off with … after two lines), with an icon first when the household has bought it before (no legend); then its price at the bottom, left-aligned ("$8.07", with "$2.69 each" in small grey text when there's more than one, "est." for weighed items), and a stepper across the tile for how many. A product not bought stays in its aisle, its tile on the grey fill, the picture faded, the item crossed out in red, "Not buying" in place of the price, and the stepper greyed out. The summary is products, the total before fees, and how many to decide.
- Tapping a product opens its own page, pushed onto the flow like a recipe, with Back (reviewed 2026-09-28, over a popup): its picture, the item in large bold type and the product under it (bought-before icon first); a group with Buy (a switch) and How many (a stepper, greyed out while Buy is off); while it's not bought, the picture is faded and the item crossed out in red, as on its tile, and nothing moves; Details (price, with "$2.69 each" or "estimate"; size; bought before, Yes or No; what the recipes need), with the meals it's for under them; and its alternatives (up to eight, each with Use, which switches to it and goes back to the list; the product it had becomes an alternative).
- The product page labels the curated choices "Recommended alternatives (#)". "More alternatives" is the final row inside that list, not a separate group above it; its saved results are excluded from the recommended count. The action pushes a separate page; Back returns to the recommendations, while Use returns to the shopping list with the chosen product (review decision, 2026-10-11).
- Items Wegmans doesn't have come first, in a red card like an aisle card ("Not at Wegmans"), each with Skip and Alternatives; Next stays off until each is skipped or has an alternative. Alternatives opens the item's page: "Curry leaves", "Wegmans doesn't have it", a Skip switch, what the recipes need, what it's for and why the meals may work without it, and its alternatives.
- A placed order's list shows its products' tiles, read-only: no × and "Qty 2" in place of the stepper.
- When the agent's work doesn't finish (meal ideas, the list, shopping), its waiting screen says so plainly and says what's kept ("We couldn't finish shopping at Wegmans this time. Your list is saved."), with Try again as a button of its own under the red notice; the error itself goes to the logs, not the screen. A list made but never shopped is shopped by itself on opening the step, like a list not yet made.

Delivery, booking, and tip (stage 3, piece 9 part 3; decided 2026-09-28, replacing the design's "only the times Instacart offers" and "Place order stays off until the day opens"):

- The Delivery step (named "Delivery") is the day and the latest time it should arrive by (an "Arrive by" row with the phone's time picker under the days, saying which window it books) ("by 6:00 PM", starting at the household's delivery time). Any day can be chosen, not only the ones Instacart offers yet (up to five days after today); the step doesn't wait on Instacart.
- The final review waits while the cart is filled and checkout read (about a minute or two, like the other waits: "Getting your total from Wegmans"), then shows the estimated total with the tip and the account's address, card, and out-of-stock setting. Checkout is read with a scheduled delivery (Instacart's default is Priority, which costs more). The total is one row (reviewed 2026-09-29: "Estimated total" and the amount, with what it's made of on one line under it, "Items $113.79 · Delivery free · Service fee $6.74 · Tip $11.38", delivery named plainly rather than Instacart's "Delivery w/ scheduled & Instacart+"; the tip rule in the note under it), and the account's is an "Instacart" group (Deliver to, Pay with, If an item is out of stock); the delivery group's Arrives row says "By 6:00 PM, or 5–7 PM if nothing earlier is left". A total is read again only when the products, their counts, the tip rule, or the delivery address change (not the dates), and when coming back to one that failed (reviewed 2026-09-29). Replacements turn out to be a setting on each item in Instacart's cart, not the account's (measured 2026-09-28).
- Approving books the order: the latest window that day ending by the chosen time (Instacart's are two hours, starting every hour), else the one before it and so on through the day, else the earliest-ending window the time falls in ("by 3:00 PM": 2–4 PM), the one window allowed to end after it. It books one day after Instacart first offers the day (a scheduled job), or right away when that's passed; until then it's waiting to book, and can still be changed (it needs approving again). Changing it makes it the household's draft again, so it can be changed only while the following order has no draft (decided 2026-09-28) and hasn't been placed (decided 2026-10-04: otherwise the draft would come before an order already placed); it can be cancelled outright until it books, draft or not. Once booked, it's locked in the app. The only time choice that comes back to the user is a day with none of those windows left: they pick another day or time. (Failures that aren't about the time, like a sign-in that expired, still order nothing and say why: requirements "Shopping list".) The total isn't held to the estimate: prices can change before booking, and the confirmation shows what was charged.
- Delivery addresses are searched in Settings as the user types, using AWS autocomplete, and the normalized address is shown for approval before saving (decided 2026-10-11). Shopping and checkout select exactly one matching saved Instacart address; missing or ambiguous matches fail. A changed destination invalidates draft shopping and totals; approved orders keep their approved destination. This supersedes the 2026-09-30 decision to use the account default.
- The household's Instacart website session is signed in from this computer (`make instacart-login`), not from the app (decided 2026-09-29: an in-app browser that hands Instacart's cookies to us could work, but it's more to build and keep working for one family, and not something Apple would take into the App Store). The review says when it's signed out.
- Instacart opens a new day's windows at midnight, five days ahead (measured 2026-09-29: at 12:08 AM Tuesday it offered today through Sunday), so a delivery on day D is first offered on D−5 and booked on D−4.
- "Waiting to book" shows on the order card's dot track: the Placed dot is the current one, not checked, until the order is booked.
- Booking never goes without an approved product. A product gone by then is swapped for the first available of its alternatives, or the booking asks, by a household setting ("If a product is gone: Use an alternative / Ask me", default Use an alternative); with no alternative left, it asks.
- Checkout is read (and, in part 3b, booked) by the agent in a headless browser, not a script of the page (decided 2026-09-29: a script breaks whenever Instacart changes a page; one approach only, never a script with an agent behind it). Our code fills the cart through Instacart's cart tool, gives the agent tools that see the page as its accessibility tree and act on it, and checks what it reports by reading the elements it points at; the tools never press Place order (booking will have its own one-shot tool, after our checks). Development books as a dry run that stops before Place order; the first real click is the household's first real order, once the app is done (decided 2026-09-28).
- Booking runs at 9:00 AM Eastern on the day it's due (decided 2026-10-01: four days before delivery, so someone is awake if it needs them); an order approved after that books right away. The scheduled run repeats every hour from 9:00 AM to 9:00 PM Eastern (decided 2026-10-04): each run starts only the orders that are due and still waiting, so a run that failed is made up an hour later with nobody involved, and its schedule is watched by the same alarms as the reminders'. An order still waiting two hours after its booking time shows as Not booked ("It didn't book on Mon, Oct 5 as planned."), with Try again, which books it through the API, not the schedule, so it works when the schedule is what's broken. Lateness sends no notification (decided 2026-10-04): a push needs our code to be running, and whenever it is, the hourly run books the order.
- Production books for real only once Nick turns it on (decided 2026-10-01: an SSM switch, off until he says go; he turned it on 2026-10-04). While it's off, booking in production runs every step up to Place order and then says "Not booked: booking at Instacart isn't turned on yet", with Try again; it never shows as booked. Development is always a dry run that shows as booked with "Dry run" as the Instacart order number, so every screen and notification can be tried.
- An approved order's chip (Home's card, its page, the screen after approving) says where it is with the store, short, with an icon (reviewed 2026-10-02): Waiting to book (an hourglass), Booking, Order placed, Pick a time (a clock), Pick a product, Not booked, Check Instacart. The order's page has an Instacart group: its status, when it books or what Instacart confirmed, and what can be done (change, cancel, try again, "It was placed" / "It wasn't placed", Open Instacart orders).
- Booking's notifications (booked, with any swaps; needs a new time; needs a product chosen; not booked; check Instacart) go to whoever approved the order, and a tap opens that order, not the draft (the tap rule for reminders and ideas above is about drafts).
- Settings (household) adds a Delivery group: Delivery time (default 6:00 PM), and Tip, a percentage of the items' total (default 10%), with Maximum tip (default $15), which the tip never goes over (both steppers); and an "If a product is gone" group (Use an alternative, the default, or Ask me: two rows, a checkmark on the chosen one).

The order lock, as decided:

- The lock belongs to one opening of the order flow on one phone, not just to the member. Opening the flow on another phone (or on the same phone after the app was closed) takes over the member's own lock at once; the phone that lost it shows "You're placing this order on another phone" with "Order here" (not in the mockup).
- While the locked screen is open, it turns into the draft by itself once the other member closes the flow or their app goes away for 2 minutes.
- Discarding the draft is refused while another member is ordering (the locked card has no Discard, as mocked).
- The locked card and screen show the app's steps, not the mockup's four, and the card says what they're doing at that step ("Sam is choosing the dates and servings"; with the one-thing-per-step flow, each step's own words).

Meal pictures (reviewed 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-07; the mockup's food illustrations):

- A meal's picture is drawn from its ingredients, not generated: the agent tags each meal with its dish (plate, bowl, skillet, or tacos) and each ingredient it pictures with one of 26 icons and one of 16 colors (`castle_chef_agent.meals`; Nick chose tagging over a lookup table in the app, 2026-10-07). Salt, oil, spices, herbs, and other small amounts aren't pictured.
- The picture (ui/art `MealArt`): the main ingredient (the first fish, shrimp, drumstick, steak, pork chop, ground meat, tofu, egg, beans, or chickpeas; else the first side) large, up and to the right, in front; under it its bed (the first rice, pasta, sauce, or tortilla, drawn as its surface; else the next ingredient); two sides behind it, the first lying along the dish's lower-left edge. All in the dish seen from above (a plate with the ring of its well, a bowl, a skillet with its handle), or tacos side-on with the tortilla as the shell. A tint of the first ingredient fills the picture's slot. No herb garnish (reviewed 2026-10-07: every meal had it).
- Icons are flat, in the app's ink line on warm fills, with no depth. Asparagus and green beans share one icon (stalks); a steak is a T-bone's teardrop with a band of fat and a round bone; pasta is five elbow macaroni.
- Meals from before pictures, and the agent's waiting screens, show the plain plate (ui/art `PlateArt`).

Cooking (decided 2026-10-04):

- A step's timer rings with a phone notification, scheduled when it starts, so it rings with the phone locked or the app closed, with a sound bundled with the app (iOS rings silently without one), and in the app too. It needs the phone's notifications; when they're off, the timer doesn't start and says how to turn them on (Nick chose a timer that rings over one counting down only while the app is open, 2026-10-04).
- One timer runs at a time; starting another replaces it. It runs until it rings or the cook taps it to stop it: closing the cooking view or tapping Done doesn't stop it (over the mockup, where going back drops it), nor does the app closing. Reopening a meal's cooking view goes back to the step it was left at (Done starts it over); after the app has closed, to its running timer's step.
- Each step says how long it waits, for its timer, and which of the recipe's ingredients it uses, for "You'll need" (amounts at the servings shown on the recipe); the agent gives both with the recipe. Meals from before have neither.
- The screen stays on while the cooking view is open, and sleeps as usual once it closes (Nick, 2026-10-04: hands are busy while cooking).
- The cooking view is full screen over the app, like the order flow, closed with its ×. Start cooking is along the bottom of a recipe opened from Orders or History (above the tab bar), not a suggestion being picked.

## Build order

One piece at a time, each through the UI with real data:

1. Create-order flow (done).
2. Home (Orders): placed orders as cards, the days they cover in the day strip, and the next order with its target and decide-by dates. Adds the household's decide-by offset (default 1 day); its screen comes with Settings. (done)
3. History and ratings: past orders and all meals with search and filters; thumbs up, thumbs down, and favorite; the prompt to rate last week's meals; and "don't show meals like this" while picking (a household meal preference, like ratings) (done)
4. Settings: household (decide-by offset, reminder lead, default servings, members, name) and personal (notification time, your name). (done)
5. Dates, meals, and servings on one view: the order flow's step 3 (requirements "Ordering flow"), with each meal's servings stored in the order. (done)
6. Notifications: the daily reminders and "ideas ready", "Allow notifications" in Settings, and the mockup's reminder text on Home and in the order flow (the strip's reminder marks, "Reminders start …, daily · yours at 8:00 AM", the "Reminders on" chip, and "Reminders start" in the next-delivery step). Each member's reminder goes out at their clock time in their phone's zone: a time skipped by a daylight saving change has no reminder that day (EventBridge Scheduler skips it), a repeated one goes out once, and a member whose zone isn't known yet (hasn't opened the app) gets none. (done)
7. The order lock: one member ordering at a time (requirements "Order lock"), with the mockup's "Sam is placing an order" card and locked flow screen. (done)
8. The one-thing-per-step flow and the shopping list, stages 1 and 2 (requirements "Ordering flow" and "Shopping list"): Servings, Delivery date, and Next delivery become steps of their own, followed by Shopping list and a final review that places the order. Leaving Servings starts the list: Python scales each picked meal's ingredients to its servings, then the agent combines them into one list grouped by aisle, in the background like meal ideas, leaving out the items assumed on hand (shown only behind an icon). Changing the meals or servings afterwards rebuilds it from scratch. The user removes items and changes quantities; the order view shows the list. Placing still calls the stub grocery service. (done)
9. Grocery stores, stage 3 (requirements "Ordering flow", "Shopping list", "Placing and status", and "Settings"): Instacart only, for our household, with the household's accounts. Designed and approved 2026-09-27, with delivery and booking changed 2026-09-28. First, a check that Instacart lets a browser in from AWS (done). Walmart is recorded as the path for other households later. In three parts:
   1. The Instacart connection: the Codex package in the worker image, the household's ChatGPT sign-in with Instacart connected (on EFS), and Instacart's search and cart tools called through the Codex app-server. (done)
   2. Shopping: after Servings, one wait makes the list and shops it (the model chooses each product and up to eight alternatives through our tools, which call Instacart's own tools); the shopping list step shows the products as tiles (a page per product; a red card for anything Wegmans doesn't have); a list not decided can't go on.
   3. Delivery, booking, and placing: the Delivery step (day and latest time); Settings' delivery time and tip; the final review's wait while the cart is filled and checkout read, then the estimated total; approving books it through a headless browser with the household's Instacart session (the latest window that day ending by the time), now or, for a day Instacart doesn't offer yet, from a scheduled job one day after Instacart first offers it, checking everything first and clicking once; "Waiting to book" on the dot track; the one case that asks the user again (no window left that day by the time); failures that order nothing, and "check Instacart" after an unconfirmed click; the confirmation with the Instacart order. (done; booking in production is on since 2026-10-04)
10. Household members from the app: adding and removing people (requirements "Household"). Designed 2026-10-03 (done):
   - Inviting records a name and an email (Settings → Members → Invite someone); the person joins when they first sign in with that email (their emailed code creates the account). Only invited emails can create an account: Neon Auth's blocking `user.before_create` webhook asks the API, which allows an email only while it has an invitation (this also closes the gap where anyone could make an empty account). Invites show as "Invited" until used, and can be cancelled.
   - Getting the app stays manual: the owner adds them as a tester in App Store Connect (TestFlight); the invite screen says they'll need it.
   - Anyone in the household can invite and remove anyone, themselves too (leaving); the last member can't be removed. Removing ends the membership and keeps the account (their name stays on past orders, and inviting them again works): they lose access at once, their phones stop getting notifications, their reminder schedule goes, and an order lock of theirs is released.
   - The `household invite` and `household remove` commands do the same (an invitation; the membership), so there's one way to add and remove people.
11. Cooking: a recipe's Start cooking opens its steps one at a time, with each step's timer and the ingredients it uses (requirements "Views": Instructions view). Designed 2026-10-04 (see "Cooking" above) (done).

## Open questions

- Home: should the current order's meals always be visible, even when another card is on top?
